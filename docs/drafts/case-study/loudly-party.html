Two problems, one app.

For the people using it

Friends, music, and nobody brought a speaker.

It started at a jam with friends in college. Nobody had a speaker, but everyone had a phone. Loudly turns those phones into one: one person hosts, everyone else joins, and the room plays as one.

Why it's hard

Press play on five phones and they drift apart in seconds.

Every phone has a different processor, scheduler and speaker delay, and ears hear 20 to 30 ms as an echo. Sonos and AirPlay solve this with their own hardware. Loudly does it in software, on ordinary phones.

A code, and the room fills up.

The host gets a six-character code. Friends type it or pick the room from the nearby list, and their phones connect straight to the host's over Wi-Fi Direct or Bluetooth. Late arrivals drop into the song mid-play.

    Liner notes.

    The calls behind the sound.

    One place owns time.

    Only the sync engine works out clock offsets. Screens never touch audio, codecs or radios, so timing bugs have one place to live.

    Never fail silently.

    When a guest drops to a slow Bluetooth link, the room says so and the stream falls from 128 to 48 kbps.

    People leave and come back.

    A guest tries three times to reconnect. The host keeps their place for 15 seconds, and they rejoin mid-song.

    Checked on real phones.

    Every change is tried on real devices with the app's own timing readings and by ear. A microphone recording is the next check.
